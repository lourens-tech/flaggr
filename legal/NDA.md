## 1. Background and Purpose

1.1 The Discloser owns and operates **Flagrr** (also written "Flaggr"), a golf loyalty platform under which members of participating golf clubs earn a loyalty currency ("Flagrr Cash") by scanning till receipts and recording rounds played, and redeem that currency for rewards, together with its mobile and web applications, backend services, databases, and related materials (the **"Project"**).

1.2 The Parties wish to discuss and explore **[DESCRIBE PURPOSE — e.g. "the Recipient's provision of software development services to the Discloser" / "the participation of the Recipient's golf club in the Flagrr programme" / "a possible investment in the Discloser" / "a possible commercial partnership"]** (the **"Permitted Purpose"**).

1.3 For that Permitted Purpose the Discloser may disclose Confidential Information to the Recipient. This Agreement governs how that information may be used and protected.

## 2. Definition of Confidential Information

2.1 **"Confidential Information"** means all information relating to the Project or to the Discloser's business, in any form and whether or not marked "confidential", that is disclosed by or on behalf of the Discloser to the Recipient before or after the Effective Date, or that the Recipient observes, accesses, derives or generates in connection with the Permitted Purpose.

2.2 Without limiting clause 2.1, Confidential Information expressly includes:

  (a) **Source code and technical materials** — the source code, object code, repositories, commit history, architecture, API design and endpoint structure, database schemas, migrations and seed data, configuration, infrastructure design, deployment arrangements and third-party service configurations;

  (b) **Proprietary logic** — the receipt-capture and optical character recognition pipeline, receipt parsing and merchant/product matching logic, duplicate-detection and image-fingerprinting methods, fraud-detection signals and thresholds, the points engine, and the streak, voucher and notification logic;

  (c) **Programme economics** — Flagrr Cash earning and conversion rates (including any Flagrr-Cash-per-Rand rate), tier thresholds, earning multipliers, bonuses, voucher values, reward catalogues and costings, pricing, subscription fees and unit economics;

  (d) **Commercial information** — business plans, product roadmap, financial information, forecasts, funding and investor materials, the identity of and commercial terms agreed with participating or prospective golf clubs, merchants, advertisers, suppliers and service providers;

  (e) **Personal Information** — any personal information of members, club staff, administrators or other individuals accessible through or contained in the Project, including names, contact details, dates of birth, authentication credentials, device tokens, submitted receipts and receipt images, transaction and spend records, and activity and redemption history; and

  (f) **Derived materials** — any notes, analyses, summaries, extracts, models, copies or other materials prepared by the Recipient to the extent they contain, reflect or are derived from any of the above.

2.3 The fact that discussions between the Parties are taking place, their status, and the terms of this Agreement are themselves Confidential Information.

## 3. Exclusions

3.1 Confidential Information does not include information that the Recipient can show by written record:

  (a) is or becomes publicly available other than through a breach of this Agreement or of any other duty of confidence;

  (b) was lawfully in the Recipient's possession, without restriction as to its use or disclosure, before it was disclosed by the Discloser;

  (c) is lawfully received by the Recipient from a third party who is free to disclose it and is not under any obligation of confidence in respect of it; or

  (d) is independently developed by the Recipient without any use of or reference to the Confidential Information.

3.2 Information does not fall within clause 3.1 merely because it is embraced by more general information that does, or because it can be assembled from a combination of separate items each of which does.

3.3 Personal Information never falls within clause 3.1 and remains subject to clause 7 in all circumstances.

## 4. Recipient's Obligations

4.1 The Recipient shall:

  (a) keep the Confidential Information strictly confidential;

  (b) use the Confidential Information solely for the Permitted Purpose and for no other purpose whatsoever, and in particular not for its own commercial advantage or that of any third party;

  (c) protect the Confidential Information using at least the same degree of care it applies to its own confidential information of a similar nature, and in no event less than a reasonable degree of care;

  (d) not copy, reproduce, publish, distribute, store or transmit the Confidential Information except to the extent strictly necessary for the Permitted Purpose;

  (e) not reverse engineer, decompile, disassemble, benchmark or otherwise attempt to derive the source code, structure, algorithms or underlying ideas of any software, application or system forming part of the Project, other than as expressly authorised in writing by the Discloser;

  (f) not develop, assist in developing, or advise any third party in relation to any product or service that replicates or is substantially derived from the Project or any part of the Confidential Information; and

  (g) notify the Discloser in writing without undue delay, and in any event within **[48] hours**, of becoming aware of any actual or suspected unauthorised use, disclosure, access, loss or compromise of any Confidential Information, and cooperate fully with the Discloser in investigating and mitigating it.

4.2 **Permitted recipients.** The Recipient may disclose Confidential Information only to those of its directors, employees, professional advisers and subcontractors who strictly need to know it for the Permitted Purpose (each a **"Representative"**), and only after informing them of its confidential nature and ensuring they are bound by obligations of confidentiality no less protective than those in this Agreement. The Recipient remains fully liable for any act or omission of its Representatives that would breach this Agreement if done by the Recipient.

4.3 **Access credentials.** Any credentials, tokens, API keys or access granted to the Recipient are personal to the Recipient, may not be shared, and must be used only for the Permitted Purpose. The Recipient shall not access any part of the Project's systems or data beyond what the Permitted Purpose requires.

## 7. Personal Information and POPIA

7.1 The Parties acknowledge that Confidential Information includes **personal information** as defined in the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (**"POPIA"**), and that the Discloser is the responsible party in respect of it.

7.2 Where the Recipient processes personal information in connection with the Permitted Purpose, it does so **only** on the documented instruction of the Discloser and as an operator for the purposes of POPIA, and shall:

  (a) process such personal information only to the extent necessary for the Permitted Purpose, and never for its own purposes, for marketing, for training any machine learning model, or for any form of profiling or resale;

  (b) secure the integrity and confidentiality of such personal information by taking appropriate, reasonable technical and organisational measures as contemplated in section 19 of POPIA, including access control, encryption in transit and at rest where reasonably practicable, and the prompt revocation of access when no longer needed;

  (c) not transfer such personal information outside the Republic of South Africa without the Discloser's prior written consent and compliance with section 72 of POPIA;

  (d) notify the Discloser immediately, and in any event within **[24] hours**, where there are reasonable grounds to believe that such personal information has been accessed or acquired by any unauthorised person, and provide all information the Discloser reasonably requires to make any notification under section 22 of POPIA; and

  (e) on termination, deal with such personal information in accordance with clause 9.

7.3 The Recipient shall not de-anonymise, re-identify or attempt to re-identify any aggregated or anonymised data derived from the Project.

## Notes for completion — delete this section before signing

- **Not legal advice.** This is a drafting template, not legal advice. Have a qualified attorney review it before use, particularly clauses 7 (POPIA), 8 (term) and 12 (non-solicitation).
- **Fill in every `[BRACKETED]` field** and delete any bracketed alternatives that do not apply.
- **One-way vs mutual:** leave the box on page 1 unticked for a one-way NDA protecting Flagrr only; tick it where the counterparty will also share its own confidential information (common with golf clubs sharing member rosters or pricing, and with investors).
- **Non-solicitation (clause 12)** is optional and is often the clause a counterparty pushes back on — delete it for low-touch counterparties such as a club evaluating the programme.
- **Assumed jurisdiction** is South Africa (Western Cape), based on the programme operating in Rand and the first participating club being in the Western Cape. Change clause 16.6 and the Party descriptions if the disclosing entity sits elsewhere.
- **If the Recipient is a developer or contractor**, this NDA does not deal with ownership of work product — you need an IP assignment in the services agreement or a separate assignment, because under South African law copyright in commissioned software vests in the author unless assigned in writing.
- **If the Recipient will access live member data**, consider whether a standalone operator agreement under section 21 of POPIA is needed in addition to clause 7.
