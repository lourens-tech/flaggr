## 1. Background and Purpose

1.1 The Company owns and operates **Flagrr** (also written "Flaggr"), a golf loyalty platform under which members of participating golf clubs earn a loyalty currency ("Flagrr Cash") by scanning till receipts and recording rounds played, and redeem that currency for rewards, together with its mobile and web applications, backend services, databases and related materials (the **"Business"**).

1.2 The Company is willing to disclose Confidential Information to the Recipient **solely so that the Recipient may evaluate a possible investment in, or financing of, the Company** (the **"Permitted Purpose"**), including conducting due diligence and considering, negotiating and documenting the terms of such an investment.

1.3 This Agreement governs how that information may be used and protected. Its purpose is to enable a candid diligence process, not to restrict the Recipient's ordinary investment activities, which clause 6 expressly preserves.

## 2. Confidential Information

2.1 **"Confidential Information"** means all information relating to the Business or the Company, in any form and whether or not marked "confidential", that is disclosed by or on behalf of the Company to the Recipient before or after the Effective Date, or that the Recipient observes, accesses, derives or generates in connection with the Permitted Purpose, including information made available through any data room, demonstration, management presentation or site visit.

2.2 Without limiting clause 2.1, Confidential Information expressly includes:

  (a) **Fundraising information** — the fact that the Company is seeking or considering investment, the status and progress of that process, proposed and agreed terms, valuation, the capitalisation table, shareholder and option arrangements, term sheets, and the identity of and terms discussed with any other actual or prospective investor;

  (b) **Financial information** — management accounts, financial statements, the financial model, budgets, forecasts, projections, cash position and runway, revenue and cost breakdowns, and unit economics;

  (c) **Commercial information** — business plans, product roadmap, go-to-market strategy, pricing and subscription terms, member and club acquisition metrics, retention and engagement data, and the identity of and commercial terms agreed with participating or prospective golf clubs, merchants, advertisers, suppliers and service providers;

  (d) **Programme economics** — Flagrr Cash earning and conversion rates (including any Flagrr-Cash-per-Rand rate), tier thresholds, earning multipliers, bonuses, voucher values, reward catalogues and the cost of funding the rewards programme;

  (e) **Technical materials** — source code, object code, repositories, architecture, API design, database schemas and migrations, infrastructure design, deployment arrangements and third-party service configurations;

  (f) **Proprietary logic** — the receipt-capture and optical character recognition pipeline, receipt parsing and merchant/product matching logic, duplicate-detection and image-fingerprinting methods, fraud-detection signals and thresholds, the points engine, and the streak, voucher and notification logic;

  (g) **Legal and corporate information** — constitutional documents, material contracts, intellectual property filings and ownership, regulatory and compliance position, disputes and contingent liabilities, and employee and contractor arrangements;

  (h) **Personal information** — any personal information of members, club staff, employees, contractors or other individuals disclosed to or accessible by the Recipient, including names, contact details, dates of birth, submitted receipts and receipt images, and transaction, spend and redemption history; and

  (i) **Derived materials** — any notes, analyses, memoranda, investment committee papers, summaries, extracts, models, copies or other materials prepared by the Recipient or its Representatives to the extent they contain, reflect or are derived from any of the above.

2.3 The existence and terms of this Agreement, the fact that discussions between the Parties are taking place, and their status and content, are themselves Confidential Information.

## 3. Exclusions

3.1 Confidential Information does not include information that the Recipient can show by contemporaneous written record:

  (a) is or becomes publicly available other than through a breach of this Agreement or of any other duty of confidence;

  (b) was lawfully in the Recipient's possession, without restriction as to its use or disclosure, before it was disclosed by the Company;

  (c) is lawfully received by the Recipient from a third party who is free to disclose it and is not under any obligation of confidence in respect of it; or

  (d) is independently developed by the Recipient or its Representatives without any use of or reference to the Confidential Information.

3.2 Information does not fall within clause 3.1 merely because it is embraced by more general information that does, or because it can be assembled from a combination of separate items each of which does.

3.3 Personal information as described in clause 2.2(h) never falls within clause 3.1 and remains subject to clause 11 in all circumstances.

## Notes for completion — delete this section before signing

**Not legal advice.** This is a drafting template, not legal advice, and it was not prepared by a lawyer. Have a qualified attorney review it before use.

**Fill in every `[BRACKETED]` field** and delete any bracketed alternatives that do not apply.

**Expect these to be negotiated.** In rough order of how often an investor will raise them:

- **Clause 7 (non-circumvention)** — many funds strike this outright as too close to a restraint of trade. It is the first clause to concede if a deal is otherwise stalling. There is deliberately no employee non-solicitation clause; institutional investors almost never accept one at NDA stage.
- **Clause 12.2 (three-year survival)** — investors frequently push for two years, or for one to three years running from disclosure rather than termination. Two years is a reasonable landing spot.
- **Clause 5.2 (co-investor consent)** — a lead investor assembling a syndicate may want to disclose to co-investors without seeking consent each time. Consider pre-approving a named list instead of refusing.
- **A "residuals" clause** — some funds ask for the right to use anything their people retain in unaided memory. This substantially weakens the agreement, particularly around programme economics and the fraud and matching logic, and should be resisted or narrowed to exclude technical materials and clause 2.2(a)–(d).
- **Mutuality** — occasionally requested where the investor shares its own fund information. Adding a reciprocal obligation is usually harmless, but the Business and clauses 9 and 10 must stay one-way in the Company's favour.

**Clause 6 is there on purpose.** A fund cannot sign anything that stops it investing elsewhere in loyalty, fintech or sports tech, so clause 6 preserves that freedom while clause 6.3 keeps it from becoming a loophole. Do not delete it to "tighten" the agreement — it is what makes the rest signable.

**Staging disclosure matters more than the NDA.** The strongest protection for the receipt-parsing and matching logic, the fraud thresholds and the Flagrr Cash economics is not to put them in the first data room. Share metrics, financials and the product demonstration first; hold source code and the fraud and matching logic for confirmatory diligence with a signed term sheet in place.

**Personal information.** Clause 11.2 requires aggregated or anonymised data by default. Investor diligence should not need member names, dates of birth or receipt images, and giving access to them creates a POPIA exposure that no NDA clause removes. If a data room will contain live member records, raise it with your attorney first.

**Some funds refuse NDAs at pitch stage entirely.** That is common and not a red flag on its own. The usual answer is to pitch without confidential material and put the NDA in place before diligence opens.
